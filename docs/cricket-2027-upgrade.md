## Free stock pipeline

Try a generic rigged human and compatible locomotion clips from [Adobe Mixamo](https://helpx.adobe.com/creative-cloud/faq/mixamo-faq.html). Download the character with skin and clips on the same skeleton. Use Three.js FBXLoader and AnimationMixer for the first prototype. Keep one loaded template and clone skinned actors with SkeletonUtils. Prefer GLB with GLTFLoader for assets supplied in that format. No Blender step is required. Stock walks cover approach and recovery. Cricket release, two-handed grip, and pickup still need authored motion. Use the existing cricket-actions poses as keyframes mapped to named stock bones if suitable licensed clips cannot be found.

Equipment candidates are [Bharath’s cricket set](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/cricket-set-03f793a975804e7680d0682e2cbf1c3c) and [Helindu’s helmet](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/cricket-helmet-d3b2cb17718f4e33b74431db3eec5732), both listed under CC Attribution. Use [Poly Haven](https://polyhaven.com/license) for CC0 turf or lighting. These are candidates pending download, rig, size and licence review. Record creator, source, licence, credit and modifications before importing. Check whether source redistribution is allowed before committing downloadable character files. Prefer an explicitly redistributable rig if publishing the source assets. Use a generic kit with no sponsor marks. Ship the first successful combination before adding variants.

## Work sequence and completion checks

1. **Measure one over.** Run npm run dev and open /?era=2027. Record browser, device, viewport, render scale, frame intervals and input-to-visible-motion during six deliveries. Capture the same over in classic for regression comparison. Proposed mobile target is a 95th percentile frame interval under 33 ms and first visible response within 100 ms. Record actual measurements alongside targets.
2. **Fit one actor.** Start in athlete.js, athlete-skin.js and cricket-scale.js. Replace only the modern striker, retaining the procedural fallback during loading or failure. Map feet, hips, wrists and bat grip. Verify the soles meet the crease, the bat reaches the pitch and both hands hold the handle on five sampled frames of a drive. Record added network bytes and repeat the baseline.
3. **Connect motion and ball.** In athlete-motion.js, cricket-actions.js and modern-cricket.js, implement one pace release, forward defence and drive. Pin the planted foot and use wrist and blade attachment points. The simulation owns the live ball after release. Scrub a delivery frame by frame and check release at the hand, contact at the blade, missed contact on a late swing, and replay agreement. Then extend to spin, pull, keeper gather and return throw.
4. **Default to assisted play.** Adapt existing shots.js, bowling-profiles.js and running.js through modern-only input handlers. Bat with one timed tap or keypress and optional directional aim. Infer footwork and stroke from line and length. Bowl with one confirmation of a suggested delivery, with optional overrides. Automatically handle field placement, chase, pickup, throw, keeper backup and clearly safe singles. Offer hold or cancel for running. Finish an over on touch and keyboard without mandatory menus between balls. Verify early and late timing can still produce distinct outcomes.
5. **Frame the decision.** In ball-camera.js, add a shoulder-offset batting view mirrored for handedness and keep the end-on bowling mount. Hold the camera through release, bounce and impact. Follow fielding once shot direction is clear. On a phone check that the batter never hides the bounce and that a tight single shows the receiving end. Repeat with a left-hander and a yorker.
6. **Finish the match presentation.** Start with ground-fielding.js, return-controller.js, modern-stadium.js, modern-crowd.js and the commentary hooks in main.js. Implement one complete drive to cover with gather, backup, throw and umpire outcome. Tie crowd and commentary timing to confirmed events. Check a grounded four, caught ball, run-out and wide with runs against the chosen match format. Compare scoreboard, strike, legal-ball count, signals and audio for each. Add pitch wear, sightscreen and crowd variation after repeating the phone performance check.

### Stock striker fit

Task 2 uses the CC BY 4.0 Cesium Man body as the first redistributable human mesh. Its procedural cricket head and equipment remain on the match skeleton. The body is local and needs no model service at runtime. The converter in `scripts/fit-striker.mjs` takes the upstream CesiumMan.glb, fits bind positions and skin weights to the cricket skeleton and omits the original texture and logo. See `public/models/CREDITS.txt` for source and licence.

The fitted body uses 1,154 triangles and adds 150,374 asset bytes before HTTP compression (about 63 KB with gzip). Its fallback is the existing procedural body. Five drive phases on each hand produced valid deformed vertices, a maximum 9.7 mm grip error and a planted sole at pitch height. Run `node --test tests/stock-striker.test.mjs` to repeat. These checks establish rig compatibility. The batter still uses the existing stylised head and cricket poses.

Task 2 checked in both editions. The modern match completed six legal deliveries with the stock body and another six with its request blocked. Classic completed an over without requesting the body. See [the five drive poses](qa/stock-striker-drive.png) and [performance results](delivery-baseline.md). The measurement target is still open. The next task is release, impact and follow-through synchronization.

Task 7 rules checkpoint. Modern matches now award a free hit after a no-ball, carry it across an illegal delivery and clear it after a legal ball or at innings change. Reference format: [ICC Men's T20I playing conditions, October 2026](https://images.icc-cricket.com/image/upload/prd/bsxcho7ufe2pjban0wjp.pdf). Short formats use the same free-hit rule. The regression suite covers no-ball, wide, catch and run-out transitions.

Task 8 dismissal checkpoint. A missed modern delivery continues under Rapier until swept contact with the wicket, pads or keeper. LBW uses the first pitch line, pad impact line, whether the striker played a shot and a short gravity-adjusted projected path. Pad shape and post-impact projection are approximations. A keeper holding the ball can attempt a stumping when the striker is outside the crease. The adjudication checks actual glove-to-wicket contact and bat/feet grounding at that instant. A stumping can count on a wide and cannot count on a no-ball or free hit. Missed-shot wicket replay appeared in the controlled Chrome match; skipping resumed scoring. Physical handset validation is pending.

Task 9 bowling increment. Pace bowlers now mix slower balls, off cutters and seam-away deliveries with their swing repertoire. Pace variations respect the bowling arm. Cutters and spin receive lateral movement on the first pitch contact. Venue presets tune bounce and turn. At a 2.2 m release, 0.65 s nominal travel and 120 Hz, first pitch positions (world z in metres) for yorker/full/good/short/bouncer were 8.13, 5.76, 1.75, −1.91 and −4.79. Heights at the striker were 0.26, 0.46, 0.72, 1.04 and 1.74 m respectively. The full toss reached the striker at 0.72 m without pitching. Opposite cutters separate after pitching at both 30 and 120 Hz.
